# Style Guide — *The Strays*

House style rails. Read alongside `Canon.md` (Canon wins on story; this wins on
prose mechanics) and **`voice.md` (the author's actual prose voice, derived from
the Chapter 1 sample — the texture these mechanics wrap around)**. These settings
are the defaults every agent applies unless the author overrides them for a
specific chapter.

> Mechanics live here; **voice lives in `voice.md`** — treat them as a pair. The
> single most important voice rule: full comic voice in the mundane, restrained
> and earnest prose in the horror.

---

## POV & tense — LOCKED

- **Point of view:** **First person, Gabriel.** The whole series is narrated by
  Gabriel as "I." We never leave his head.
- **Tense:** **Past tense** ("I walked," "I saw") — the default for the series.
  A chapter may use present tense only if the author explicitly asks.
- **Single narrator:** No POV switches, no chapters from Rebecca or anyone else,
  no omniscient cut-aways. If something happens where Gabriel isn't present, he
  can only learn it secondhand — and secondhand accounts are as deniable as
  everything else.

### What first-person costs us (enforce these)

- **Perception limit:** the page contains only what Gabriel sees, hears, smells,
  feels, remembers, or infers. No fact enters the story except through him.
- **The unreliable "I":** Gabriel is the ambiguity engine in first person. He can
  misperceive, misremember, lose time, and narrate things that may not have
  happened. Lean into this — but never *tell* the reader "I was hallucinating."
  Let the gaps and contradictions do the work.
- **No filter-word crutch:** because everything is already his perception, avoid
  padding every image with "I saw / I heard / I felt / I noticed." Render the
  thing directly; reserve the filter verb for when the *act* of perceiving is the
  point (e.g. straining to hear, doubting his own eyes).
- **Interiority is the medium, not a garnish:** his thoughts, doubts, and
  rationalizations are the horror. But keep it earned — dread over rumination.

---

## Voice

- Gabriel's narrating voice: intelligent, observant, self-questioning. A man
  trying to stay reasonable while reason slips. Dry, occasionally wry, never
  quippy. He explains things to himself to stay calm — and it doesn't work.
- Grounded and concrete. Specific nouns. Real domestic texture before the wrong.
- Restraint: understate the horror. Short, flat sentences when it lands hardest.

---

## Sentence & prose mechanics

- Vary sentence length; let rhythm carry tension. Long, careful sentences for
  ordinary observation; short fragments when something breaks.
- Prefer active, physical verbs. Cut adverbs that prop up weak verbs.
- Dialogue: naturalistic, sparing, subtext-carrying. People rarely say the true
  thing directly — least of all in Redding.
- Paragraphs: white space is a tool. Isolate the wrong beat on its own line.
- **Em dashes: use sparingly — a hard cap of ~1–2 per chapter.** Default to commas,
  parentheses, colons, or a new sentence for asides. Overusing the em dash is a
  banned tic (see the punctuation rule in `voice.md`).

---

## Formatting conventions

- Chapters are prose only (see the prose agent). An optional `# Title` line is
  allowed at the top.
- Dialogue in standard double quotes. New speaker, new paragraph.
- Gabriel's remembered/imagined material is woven into narration, not set in
  special typography, unless the author establishes a convention later.

---

## Length

- Per chapter: **5,000–8,000 characters** (letters incl. spaces). Hard range,
  enforced by the prose agent.
