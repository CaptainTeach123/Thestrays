# Format — *The Strays* as an Epistolary Dossier

The novel is presented as a **recovered dossier**: Gabriel's private journal,
assembled by an unnamed editor together with corroborating documents (texts,
emails, screenshots, notes) retrieved from the Redding house. This is the top
authority on *form*; read it alongside `voice.md` (texture) and `style-guide.md`
(mechanics). The story, canon, characters, and voice are all unchanged — only the
container is new.

Why this form fits the story: a journal is subjective, gapped, and written by a
man who cannot trust himself, so it is unreliable *by construction*. The inserted
documents let the reader weigh evidence directly. Both serve the two-readings
engine.

---

## The framing device

- **Editor's note** opens the book once (see `manuscript/00-editors-note.md`). An
  antiquarian, tight-lipped editor presents the pages as recovered, makes no claim
  as to their truth, and marks damage and gaps.
- The editor may interject sparingly in brackets between entries to note missing,
  damaged, struck-out, or illegible material. Keep it rare and dry; the editor
  never explains the story, only the state of the paper.

### The editor as a character

The editor is a real (if minor) presence in the book, not just a formatting
device. Treat him as a person with the following shape:

- **Who he is:** an unnamed antiquarian/archivist by trade (which suits the
  leather-bound object the book is). The journal and its loose papers came into
  his keeping; he has arranged and lightly annotated them. He signs "— Ed." and is
  never named.
- **Voice:** spare, formal, careful, a little old-fashioned. Understatement to the
  point of dryness. He states what the paper is and what he did or didn't verify.
  He does not emote; his unease shows only in what he chooses to mention and in the
  things he declines to say.
- **What he does:** notes the physical state of the manuscript; occasionally
  cross-references a claim against ordinary public record and reports the result
  flatly; arranges documents so the reader meets facts as the writer did.
- **His arc (subtext only):** across the book the manuscript works on him too. His
  early notes are purely archival; later ones betray, without ever admitting it,
  that he has stopped being able to hold the pages at arm's length. Keep this
  strictly between the lines. He never says "I am frightened."

**Hard rules for editor interjections:**

- **Never resolve the two-readings engine.** The editor may deepen the ambiguity
  but must never confirm the supernatural OR confirm Gabriel is ill. A cross-
  reference should cut both ways (a record that's merely absent, a photo that's
  merely "of someone"), never settle it.
- **Never explain the story or the lore.** No theories, no naming the Rusalka or
  the double, no interpreting Gabriel. He reports; he does not analyze.
- **Don't invent major new plot.** He works with the manuscript and mundane
  record. Large canonical facts (e.g. whether Craig officially exists) are the
  author's to decide, not the editor's to assert.
- **Stay rare.** At most one to three short interjections per chapter, plus the
  gap/damage marks. The journal is the book; the editor is a thin, cold frame
  around it.
- **In-world reason Gabriel keeps a journal:** he is a writer, off his pills and
  sleepless, using the page as the one confidant he can't disappoint or frighten.
  He says as much early. It doubles as his coping mechanism and our unreliable lens.

## Building blocks

**1. Journal entries** — the spine. Gabriel's first-person, past-tense voice,
exactly as in `voice.md`. Headed by a date line (see below). Entries are written
*after* the events, so the retrospective-foreshadow move still works ("I did not
know then…"). Most of each chapter's word count lives here.

**2. Inserted documents** — accents between or inside entries. Label each on its
own line in brackets, then the content. Keep them short and real:

- `[TEXT — Rebecca, 11:58pm]` a message, lowercase, texting register
- `[EMAIL — Redding General, Staff Announcements]` subject + a line or two
- `[HOSPITAL STAFF DIRECTORY — screenshot]` a caption describing what's shown
- `[WEATHER — saved screenshot, 1:14am]` e.g. "Clear. 0% precipitation."
- `[NOTE — clipped to the fridge]` a handwritten scrap, a vet note, a receipt

**3. Editor's marks** — the deterioration/gap device, in brackets, italic-plain:

- `[The next several pages are water-damaged and illegible. — Ed.]`
- `[Two lines here are struck through so heavily the paper is torn. — Ed.]`
- `[No entry for the following eleven days. — Ed.]`

## Structure of a chapter

- Chapters remain the unit (same filenames: `episode-NN-ch-01.md`). Inside, a
  chapter is a **cluster of dated entries plus documents**, in reading order.
- A chapter may open with a short **undated fragment** as a hook before the dated
  entries resume (e.g. Ch.1 opens on the overlook fragment). Use sparingly.
- Prefer 2–4 entries per chapter over one long one; white space between documents
  is part of the dread.

## Dates & the deterioration arc

- Use modern day/month date lines (`17 June`), occasionally a time for night
  entries. Keep the timeline consistent with the canon (summer; Ch.1 move,
  Ch.2 ~a month on, Ch.3 a few weeks after that).
- **The form decays with Gabriel.** Early chapters: regular, dated, composed
  entries. As the season darkens (per `voice.md`'s humor-decay arc): entries get
  shorter and more erratic, dates go missing or wrong, the editor marks more gaps,
  the handwriting is noted as changing, documents start contradicting the journal.
  The falling-apart of the *form* mirrors the falling-apart of the *man*.

## Punctuation note (em dashes)

The `voice.md` em-dash cap (~1–2 per chapter) applies to the **journal prose
only**. Em dashes used as separators in **document labels and editor's marks**
(`[TEXT — Rebecca]`, `— Ed.`) are structural formatting and don't count against
the budget. Don't let the label style leak em dashes back into the narrative prose.

## What does NOT change

- POV: first-person Gabriel, single narrator (documents from others are *quoted
  evidence*, never a POV switch — we still only know what Gabriel knows/collects).
- All canon secrets and forbidden rules stand (Rusalka unnamed; Craig's doubling
  ambiguous and never named; deniable footprints; two-readings rule).
- Voice, humor decay, and the 5,000–8,000-character-per-chapter target.
- Documents must stay deniable too: a text or photo can deepen ambiguity but must
  never be the clean corroboration that collapses it.
